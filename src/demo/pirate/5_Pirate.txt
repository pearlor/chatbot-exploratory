**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Serves:** 2  

Ahoy, Matey! Welcome to my galley! *Lulu, my African Grey parrot, flutters her wings and squawks, "Batter up! Pieces of eight!"* Hush now, Lulu, we’ve got a hungry Sailor on deck! 

If there's one thing years of sailing the seven seas has taught me, it's how to whip up a feast when the pantry is looking bare. My parents made their fortune delivering candy canes to ports far and wide, so I've always had a bit of a sweet tooth. 

Once, we were caught in a dead calm near Cuba. The crew was hot, cranky, and ready to mutiny. I slinked down into the hold and found only a few bananas, a block of butter, a sack of brown sugar, some cinnamon, and, of course, a bottle of the captain's finest dark rum. I whipped up this sweet treat, and by blackbeard's ghost, those angry scallywags were singing sea shanties and hugging the rigging by sunset!

Here is my legendary five-ingredient dessert to save any voyage!

## Cane's Butter-Rum Bananas

### [Ingredients] The Captain's Five-Item Loot

*   3 **firm, ripe bananas**
*   3 tablespoons of **salted butter**
*   4 tablespoons of **dark brown sugar**
*   1/2 teaspoon of **ground cinnamon**
*   2 tablespoons of **dark rum** (or apple juice if ye be a dry sailor!)

### [Steps] How to Cook Up the Booty

1. Peel those bananas and slice 'em right down the middle, then cut 'em in half so ye have four lovely pieces per banana.
2. Melt your **salted butter** in a skillet over medium heat till it starts to bubble like the warm Caribbean surf.
3. Stir in the **dark brown sugar** and **ground cinnamon**, cooking for about 2 minutes until it turns into a thick, bubbly caramel syrup.
4. Gently lay your **banana** slices into the pan. Let 'em sizzle for 2 minutes on one side, then flip 'em like a coin and cook for another minute until they start to soften and turn a beautiful golden brown.
5. Pour in the **dark rum**! Let it bubble away for 1 minute until the alcohol burns off, leaving only the rich, sweet flavor of the islands coating your bananas. Serve warm!

### [Pros and Cons] The Good and the Salty

* + Incredibly fast to whip up when the sea gets rough and hunger strikes.
* + Uses simple ingredients that survive long voyages without spoiling.
* + Tastes like pure gold, especially if ye can scoop some vanilla ice cream on top!
* - It vanishes quicker than a pirate's share of loot in Tortuga.
* - You have to watch the sugar closely so it doesn't burn to the bottom of the pan like black tar.
